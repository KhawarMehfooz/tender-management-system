# Overview

_Last updated: 28/08/2026_

## What is the Tender Management System

The Tender Management System (TMS) is a tool for teams that regularly bid on public and private tenders. It gives a team one shared place to track every tender from the moment it's found until it's won, lost, or dropped, and to manage the people and work involved in preparing a bid.

Instead of tenders being tracked in spreadsheets, email threads, and personal notes, TMS keeps one record per tender. That record holds its details, its current stage, who's responsible for it, what tasks still need to be done, and a full history of how it got there.

## Who it's for

TMS is built for organizations that handle tenders as an ongoing part of their business. For example, service providers bidding on public contracts in categories such as security, cleaning, or facility management. It's used by:

- **Team leads and department heads**, who assign people to tenders, oversee the pipeline, and make sure deadlines aren't missed.
- **Team members** (calculation, concept writing, documentation, quality control, and similar roles), who work on the tasks assigned to them for a given tender.
- **Administrators**, who manage user accounts, roles, and the reference data (service categories, sources, CPV/NUTS codes) the system runs on.

Access is scoped by service category. A person working in one category, cleaning for example, only sees tenders in that category, unless they hold a management-level role that spans all categories.

## The core concept: a tender's lifecycle

Every tender in TMS moves through a defined sequence of stages, from first being found to its final outcome:

**intake → review → decision → processing → quality → submission → follow-up**

ending in one of: **won, lost, cancelled, not evaluated,** or **excluded**.

A tender can only move forward one stage at a time. There's no skipping ahead and no jumping backward. Every stage change is recorded, including who made it, when, and why, so there's always a clear history of how a tender progressed. This gives every tender a predictable, auditable path, and gives the team a shared answer to "where does this stand right now."

## What you can do with it

- **Track tenders** with full details, including contracting authority, deadlines, contract value, location, and classification codes, all in one record.
- **Assign a team** to each tender: an owner responsible for it, plus team members covering specific functions (calculation, concept, evidence documents, quality control, final approval).
- **Break work into tasks**, each with an owner, a priority, a due date, and its own status. Tasks can depend on one another, so work that isn't ready yet is visibly blocked until its dependencies are done.
- **Collaborate on tasks** with comments and file attachments, so context and evidence stay attached to the work itself.
- **Get notified** when something relevant happens, such as a task's status changing, a comment being added, or a file being uploaded, with control over which of those also arrive by email.
- **Control access by role and by right.** Roles decide what a user can navigate to. Individually grantable rights (for example, seeing prices or margins) control what data they can see, enforced on every request, not just hidden in the interface.
- **Keep a clean, honest record.** Tenders are never silently deleted. They're archived or flagged invalid instead, keeping the historical record intact. Only administrators can permanently remove a genuine junk entry, and doing so is logged with a reason.
